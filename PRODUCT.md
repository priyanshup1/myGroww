# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users
First the owner: a salaried person in India who sends money to family each month, spends on a debit card and a credit card, and invests through SIPs. Next, other salaried Indians in a similar position. Logs on a phone in seconds, reviews and plans on a laptop.

## Product Purpose
Show where money goes, how to spend less, what is invested, what the future looks like 1 to 5 years out, and how to save for a car, bike or house. Success: the owner uses it regularly and trusts its numbers.

## Positioning
Family support comes out of income before anything else, so the product starts from what is truly left: what is safe to spend now, and how each rupee kept moves a named goal closer.

## Operating Context
Monthly salary, money sent to family, SIP debits, card bills, UPI and card spending. Debit trackers and card statements are imported. All data stays in the browser.

## Capabilities and Constraints
Home, Activity, Plan and Wealth areas. Quick entry, budgets, recurring bills, statement import, SIP tracking, emergency fund, goal planner with user-chosen assumptions, monthly review, installable app. Rupee amounts use Indian digit grouping. The app does maths on the user's assumptions and never recommends specific funds. There is no server, so reminders with the app closed go through the phone's calendar. Sync and accounts are undecided.

## Brand Commitments
The name myGroww is the current working name. The chosen themes are Obsidian Gold and Midnight Emerald, each with dark and light modes and one accent colour. It must feel premium and alive, with real motion and visibly connected cards. It must not be rainbow-coloured, dull like a bank app, or crowded on one screen.

## Evidence on Hand
No customers, testimonials or benchmarks exist. Do not invent them. Real financial data must never appear in the repository, tests or published pages.

## Product Principles
1. Answer "how much can I safely spend?" before anything else.
2. Logging takes seconds, or it will not happen.
3. One source of truth: every number on every screen agrees.
4. One clear thing per screen; detail appears on demand.
5. Facts and options, not financial advice.
